# x402 in TEESwap

How TEESwap takes payment by x402, and why it works the way it does. The
protocol itself is digested in `reference/X402-SPEC.md`. TEESwap implements the
MCP and HTTP transports as written.

## Funding a job

A job's inputs reach the accounts it controls in one of three ways, chosen
**when it's quoted**, because each constrains what can be quoted. The invoice
records the method and it can't change afterwards:

| Funding | Quote | Accept |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit: the client sends the inputs to the job's accounts | `teeswap_quote` | `teeswap_accept` |
| x402: the client pays, a facilitator settles into the job's account | `teeswap_quote_x402` | `teeswap_accept_x402` (paid) |
| Keys: the inputs are accounts whose keys the client hands over | `teeswap_quote_keys` (blind-only) | `teeswap_accept` |

Each tool takes one input type and returns one output type. An accept only takes
quotes of its own funding method, and says which tool to use instead.

x402 funding has two constraints: **one input token**, since a payment settles
one `accepts` entry, and **tokens only**, since x402 schemes move tokens and
native ETH can't be paid this way. TEESwap uses the `exact` scheme with EIP-3009
tokens (USDC).

Whatever the method, the job starts with its inputs in its own accounts, so
everything after that is the same.

## The paid tool

`teeswap_accept_x402` is the one paid tool:

1. **Unpaid call.** It answers with x402's `PaymentRequired`, with one `accepts`
   entry: the input's amount and token, paid to the input's account, with the
   token's EIP-712 domain in `extra`. A quote already accepted or expired says
   so instead: there's nothing to pay.
2. **Paid retry.** The same call, with the payment. The payment is checked
   inside the TEE (below), handed to a facilitator, and watched until it's final.
3. **Result.** The accept response, with the settlement. Settling the payment is
   what delivers the job's input, so the job starts with it held.

Asking to be paid is a refusal, like the tools' other errors, and x402 treats it
as one: an `isError` result over MCP, a `402` over HTTP. So a paid tool raises it
and each transport renders it in its own form, adding the `resource` (only the
transport knows where the call was made). A paid result carries its settlement,
which each transport reports its own way. Every other tool ignores a payment
sent to it, so such a payment is never submitted anywhere and never settles.

| | MCP | HTTP (REST) |
|---|---|---|
| Payment in | `params._meta["x402/payment"]` | `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE` header |
| Payment required | `isError` result: `structuredContent`, and the same JSON in `content[0].text` | `402`, `PAYMENT-REQUIRED` header |
| Settlement | `_meta["x402/payment-response"]` | `PAYMENT-RESPONSE` header |

## Checked inside the TEE

A payment is handed to a facilitator only if it pays exactly what was asked:

- its `accepted` is exactly the requirements Mai derives from the invoice (Mai
  sends the facilitator her own requirements, never the client's);
- the authorization pays exactly the input's amount, to the input's account;
- its signature recovers to its payer under the domain of the token that was
  asked for (a payment signed for another token is refused, even when it claims
  to be the right one);
- it stays valid no longer than `maxTimeoutSeconds`.

Facilitators are third parties chosen by the operator. Checking first means
nothing but an honest payment ever leaves the TEE, and a payer can't use Mai to
probe facilitators or disturb how they're ranked. The facilitator still checks
what only it can, such as the payer's balance.

## The result is final

A facilitator that errors or times out may still have submitted the payment,
and the payer's authorization stays usable until its `validBefore`. So the paid
call only answers once the answer can't change:

- **the funds are in the input's account**: success, whether or not a
  facilitator reported the transaction (if none did, `transaction` is empty);
- **the authorization can never be used**: the chain is past `validBefore` with
  its nonce unused, or no facilitator was ever asked to settle it. Only then is
  payment asked for again, with why (including what each facilitator said), so
  paying again is always safe.

Both are single state reads (a balance, the token's `authorizationState`), never
a search of past transfers. Time is the chain's, not the host's: the operator
controls the host's clock. `maxTimeoutSeconds` is short (60s), which bounds how
long a call can wait when facilitators are failing.

## Facilitators

### Selection

Facilitators are interchangeable for a given payment: x402 defines no
facilitator fees, so the only questions are whether one can settle it and
whether it's up. The candidates are the configured facilitators the monitor
last saw up that list the payment's scheme and network. Mai tries them in turn,
best first.

Best means most reliable lately. An error or a failed settlement counts against
a facilitator, a settlement for it. Reporting a payment invalid counts neither
way: the payment is someone else's, and whoever sends one mustn't be able to move
a facilitator up or down. The record fades with a half-life, so a failure last
week no longer counts, and it starts from an assumed success, so a new or rarely
used facilitator isn't penalised for being quiet. Candidates are ranked by how
they did for this job, then for every job, then by config order.

A failure never removes a facilitator, it only moves it down. Failures are often
transient (a facilitator out of gas, a network blip), and with one facilitator,
removing it would leave a job, or a payer paying again, with none at all. What
stops retrying is the job's own limits (see `EXECUTION.md`) and, for a payment,
the payer.

### Monitoring

Each facilitator is polled for `/supported`, which every facilitator must serve,
for liveness and what it can settle (a `/health` endpoint isn't part of the
interface, and varies). One that doesn't answer is down, keeps what it was last
known to settle, and is polled again soon. While every facilitator that could
settle something is down, jobs wait rather than give up: nothing is ruled out
for good by a blip.

### Trust

The operator chooses the facilitator list. A facilitator can refuse or delay a
settlement but can't redirect it: the signed authorization fixes the token,
amount, sender and recipient.

## With Verifiable MCP

- **`_meta` is shared.** A result's `_meta` carries `x402/payment-response` next
  to the verifiable-tools block; a request's `params._meta` carries
  `x402/payment` next to the verifiable-tools nonce.
- **Attestation** commits to `content`, and a payment-required result carries its
  `PaymentRequired` in `content[0].text` as the spec requires, so it's attested
  like any other result.
- **Two kinds of `isError`.** x402 results carry a `PaymentRequired` in
  `structuredContent`; other tool errors carry a message, and their error class
  for clients to raise.

## Configuration

Facilitators are part of the operator config (the `teeswap` key of the
container-init JSON). Setting `facilitators` replaces the default list.

```json
{
  "teeswap": {
    "facilitators": [
      {"url": "https://my-facilitator.example.com", "poll_interval": 300}
    ]
  }
}
```

`teeswap x402-status <url>` tests a facilitator the way the monitor does.
